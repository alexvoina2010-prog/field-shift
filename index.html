      What this tool can't see (applies to all options)
      • Your field itself. Climate comes from a NASA grid cell about 50 km wide. Hollows, frost pockets and local storms are averaged away, and model data can run hotter or drier than a nearby weather station.
      • Your soil. Only the soil type and slope you picked are used. A soil test (organic matter, pH, compaction) would change the picture.
      • Irrigation. The replay is rain-fed. If you irrigate, the water count is shown but left out of the total.
      • Money. Income is not estimated: no yields, prices, contracts, subsidies, machinery or labour costs.
      • Pests, diseases and weeds actually present. Only general rules on how often a crop should return.
      • Local crop calendars and varieties. Sowing dates, flowering months and heat limits are assumptions for temperate Europe, not your varieties.

      How it works

      Field Shift replays the weather NASA recorded for your field since 2000 against your rotation and a few alternatives, and counts how often each would have been in trouble. It supports, it does not replace, your judgment or a local agronomist.

      Data and method

      • Climate: NASA POWER daily data 2000–2025 (temperature, rainfall, solar radiation, wind, humidity, root-zone soil wetness), from NASA's MERRA-2 and CERES products.
      • Map layers: NASA GIBS: MODIS Terra NDVI (monthly), SMAP Level-4 root-zone soil moisture, GPM IMERG precipitation rate.
      • Place search: OpenStreetMap Nominatim. Only the place name you type is sent.
      • Crop water demand: FAO-56 Penman-Monteith reference evapotranspiration multiplied by FAO-56 crop coefficients, run day by day through an FAO-56 root-zone water balance. Soil water capacity comes from the soil type (FAO-56 Table 19) and the crop's rooting depth (Table 22); runoff on heavy-rain days uses the USDA curve number method.
      • "Short of water at flowering": on at least half the days of the crop's flowering months, dry soil would have let it draw less than half the water it needed (FAO-56 stress coefficient Ks below 0.5). Both halves are adjustable defaults, not benchmarks.
      • "Heatwave at flowering": 5 or more days above the crop's heat limit while it flowers.
      • Irrigation: the replay is rain-fed. "Sometimes" and "Fully irrigated" are treated the same: the field counts as irrigated, so the water count is shown but left out of the total.
      • "What matters most": a picked priority counts three times as much as one not picked. "Fine-tune" shows and changes the exact weights.
      • "N of 10 seasons OK": the 0–100 score divided by ten and rounded.
      • Confidence badge: Low when a water-balance parameter of any crop in the rotation (crop coefficient, stage lengths, rooting depth, depletion fraction) is an assumption, when the field lies outside 35°–60° latitude, or when a crop has fewer than 15 replayed seasons. Medium when all water-balance parameters are FAO-56 table values. High is not reachable yet: it needs sourced flowering months and heat limits and a second rainfall source agreeing within 20%.
      • Soil organic matter is a qualitative note from months of bare soil and cover crops, not a measurement or a score.
      • Trends: Mann-Kendall test with Sen's slope on one value per year. "How sure: strong" means p is below 0.05; "weak" means p between 0.05 and 0.1; otherwise no trend is claimed.
      • Heavy-rain days: days with 10 mm or more (the standard R10mm index).
      • Crop table: data/crops.json. Crop coefficients, stage lengths, rooting depths and depletion fractions are FAO-56 table values. Sowing and flowering months, heat limits and return intervals are assumptions with low confidence, and are labelled as such.
      • Scores: five 0–100 scores per rotation, each a count over replayed seasons or years, combined as a weighted average using your priorities. The formulas are in src/model/rotation.js.
      • Stress test "Hotter and 20 % drier": every day 1.5 °C warmer and every rainfall 20 % smaller.

      Words used on this page

      Evapotranspiration (ET)
      Water leaving the field: evaporated from the soil plus drawn up and released by plants. "Reference ET" is how much a well-watered grass field would lose in that weather.
      Root-zone soil moisture
      How wet the soil is in the top metre, where roots drink. 0% is bone dry, 100% is saturated.
      NDVI
      A satellite measure of how green and dense plants are. High on a growing crop, low on bare soil or stubble.
      Satellite and model data versus ground measurements
      Satellites and weather models cover the whole planet evenly but in coarse squares (here about 50 km). A rain gauge or soil probe in your field is more exact for that spot. Use both: this tool for the long pattern, your own records for the detail.
      Legumes (nitrogen-fixing crops)
      Peas, soybean, chickpea, vetch. Bacteria on their roots take nitrogen from the air, so they need little nitrogen fertiliser and leave some for the next crop.
      Cover crops
      Sown between cash crops to keep the soil covered. They protect against erosion, feed soil life and add organic matter, but they also use water.
      Deep-rooting crops
      Sunflower, rapeseed, sorghum. Their roots open channels that help water soak in and reach moisture other crops cannot.
      Drought-tolerant crops
      Crops such as sorghum or chickpea that keep yielding with less water or more heat, lowering the chance of a failed season.
